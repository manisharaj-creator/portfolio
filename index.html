IMMUNOLOGY / BREAST CANCER

01 ALBERT EINSTEIN COLLEGE OF MEDICINE

Exploring a stronger immune
response to breast cancer

I investigated combining an NKT-cell activator with a Listeria-based cancer vaccine during my doctoral research.

My role
Studied the combination and conducted immunological analyses.
Outcome
First-author publication reporting improved vaccine efficacy in a preclinical breast cancer model.
Inside the research +

The question: Could activating NKT cells improve the performance of a Listeria-based breast cancer vaccine?

In plain language: NKT cells are part of the immune system. This work studied a way to stimulate them alongside an experimental vaccine. The findings concern a preclinical model, rather than an established treatment for people.

MOLECULAR IMAGING / PROSTATE CANCER

02 MEMORIAL SLOAN KETTERING CANCER CENTER

Making a different cancer
signal visible

I worked on a PET tracer to image androgen receptor-independent lesions in metastatic prostate cancer.

My role
Research Fellow contributing to tracer development, interpretation, and scientific communication.
Outcome
Research presented at the World Molecular Imaging Congress.
Inside the research +

The question: How could molecular imaging help identify prostate cancer lesions that do not depend on androgen receptor signaling?

In plain language: A PET tracer is a labeled substance used to make a biological target visible on a scan. This project explored a tracer for a particular form of metastatic prostate cancer.

The supplied CV lists the associated manuscript as in preparation; its current publication status is unconfirmed.

Conference presentation · World Molecular Imaging Congress, 2016
CELL BIOLOGY / PROTEIN TRAFFICKING

03 CALIFORNIA PACIFIC MEDICAL RESEARCH INSTITUTE

Following a protein
to an unexpected place

I investigated the movement of beta-dystroglycan, a cell-surface protein, into the cell nucleus.

My role
Used GFP-fusion protein tracking and cell fractionation to study nuclear trafficking.
Outcome
Co-author of a peer-reviewed paper describing a distinctive trafficking pattern.
Inside the research +

The question: How does beta-dystroglycan move into the nucleus?

In plain language: Fluorescent protein labeling helps researchers follow where a protein goes. Cell fractionation separates parts of a cell so researchers can examine where proteins are found.

02 / MY STORY

One purpose.
New possibilities.

India → USA (California) → The Netherlands → USA (New York)

A scientific journey shaped by curiosity,
care, and a willingness to begin again.

Contributing to a cure for cancer has guided my scientific life.

I’m Dr. Manisha Singh, a cancer research scientist, Cornell Executive MBA candidate, and entrepreneur building Shyaama, an emerging lifestyle and wellness venture.

Research taught me to ask questions, examine evidence carefully, challenge assumptions, and persist when answers are unclear. Today, I’m building the business understanding to help promising scientific ideas become useful products, services, and organizations.

I care about making complex knowledge understandable without losing its meaning. Across research, learning, and entrepreneurship, I return to one question: how can knowledge improve people’s lives?

Meditation and spirituality are also part of my life, influencing my sense of purpose and service.
